# SCOPE

A title can be `true` only if the title itself clearly establishes one of:

1. **Security/SecOps:** Security Engineer/Analyst; SOC/Security Operations; SIEM/detection/monitoring; endpoint security; incident response; threat hunting/intelligence; vulnerability management; cybersecurity systems/security-operations engineering; closely related security specialties.
2. **Enterprise/Remote IT Support:** technical/IT/enterprise/remote/systems/endpoint/desktop/service support; support-oriented systems administration; clinical/healthcare IT support; closely equivalent enterprise-support functions.

Out of scope unless the title itself anchors one of the above: generic engineering; forward-deployed, field, customer, solutions, cloud, platform, dedicated network, software, data, AI/ML, automation, manufacturing, or industrial engineering; architecture; unrelated technical leadership; facilities/low-voltage work; data-center logistics; sales; account management; unrelated consulting.

Ordinarily fail as generic/domain-ambiguous: `Engineer 1`, `Engineer II`, `Technical Engineer`, `Field Engineer`, `Forward Deployed Engineer`, `Customer Engineer`, `Tech Lead`, `Technical Lead`, domain-ambiguous `Systems Engineer`, `Platform Engineer`, `Cloud Engineer`. Never infer a family merely because relevant duties are conceivable.

Modifiers may refine an in-scope occupation but never convert an out-of-scope one: `Cloud Security Engineer` may qualify, `Cloud Engineer` does not automatically; `Security Solutions Engineer` may when Security defines the occupation, `Solutions Engineer` alone does not; `Network Security Engineer` may, `Network Engineer` alone does not automatically.

Never rescue a failed scope gate with transferable experience, Resume keywords, certifications, adjacent technologies, historical duties, favorable assumptions, speculative progression, or imagined JD duties; absence of incompatibility is not positive evidence. JD review may resolve details within a known family but must never be needed to discover the broad occupational field.

## 2. Scope gate — the title alone must clearly establish a target family before Resume evidence matters; else `false` (SCOPE above).

## 3. ## 4. Resume evidence — documented evidence only, weighted: (1) professional responsibilities/accomplishments; (2) repeated/sustained occupational functions; (3) recent roles/career progression; (4) prior titles/demonstrated scope; (5) technologies/methods used professionally; (6) skills, certifications, summaries, isolated keywords. Recent/repeated professional work outweighs isolated historical duties; historical duties may support an established family but never create one; skills, certifications, frameworks, technologies, or keywords alone never establish professional experience.

## 5. Alignment — require substantial direct occupational overlap, functional equivalence, or close same-family adjacency, e.g.: threat hunting↔threat intelligence; security operations↔endpoint security; security analyst↔security engineer; vulnerability remediation↔vulnerability management; enterprise technical support↔technical support engineering; endpoint support↔systems support; enterprise support↔support-oriented systems administration. Never cross-family extrapolation, e.g.: asset management↛data-center logistics; physical access-control administration↛low-voltage technician; scripting/automation↛AI engineer; enterprise deployment↛AI deployment specialist; customer support↛cloud customer engineer; infrastructure support↛platform engineer; technical deployment↛forward-deployed engineer. A credible transferable-skill story is insufficient. Career progression may strengthen an in-scope match but never creates a family: `true` requires BOTH the destination occupation in a target family AND documented substantial direct or close same-family support — plausibility alone never suffices.

## 6. Specialization — for in-family titles, classify the specialization as directly supported, closely adjacent, unsupported-but-non-disqualifying, or materially divergent. An unfamiliar product/framework/technology/neutral modifier never disqualifies an otherwise supported occupation: supported `Security Engineer` + unfamiliar security product may pass; + RMF/NIST may pass with broader security/risk evidence; `Threat Intelligence Analyst` may pass via substantial professional threat-hunting/security-analysis evidence. Reject when an occupation-defining specialization is a substantially different professional domain lacking substantial direct or closely adjacent evidence. General cybersecurity experience does not establish OT/ICS security, malware reverse engineering, application security, offensive security/penetration testing, cryptography, or highly specialized cloud-security architecture. The more strongly specialization defines the occupation, the more direct/adjacent evidence required.

## 7. Seniority — assess from documented prior titles, relevant duration, progression, responsibility scope, ownership, technical depth, and leadership/management evidence. Never infer people management, executive leadership, principal-level expertise, architecture ownership, or organization-wide strategic authority. Employer leveling varies; tolerate modest uncertainty and reject only clear incompatibility. Seniority uncertainty never rescues a failed scope gate.

## 8. Ambiguity — in-family ambiguity (family clear; scope/specialization/level uncertain, e.g., `Senior Security Analyst`) may pass with sufficient Resume support at reduced confidence; domain ambiguity → `false`.

`true` only if ALL hold: no hard exclusion; title clearly in a target family; substantial direct overlap, functional equivalence, or close same-family adjacency; seniority reasonably plausible; no occupation-defining specialization materially unsupported. Lexical overlap (`Security`, `Technical`, `Systems`, `Cloud`, `Network`, `Engineer`, `Analyst`) without occupational alignment → `false`.
