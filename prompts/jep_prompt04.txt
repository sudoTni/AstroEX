Analyze the provided Job Description (`targJD`) against the candidate's Resume (`myResume`) and Testimonials (`myTestimonials`) to determine whether the opportunity is very highly aligned with the candidate's background, preferences, interview readiness, and likelihood of offer conversion.

# INTERNAL STAGE 2 — COMPATIBILITY ASSESSMENT

## SCORING FRAMEWORK

Total weighted score: **100 points**

A role qualifies as very highly aligned only when:

1. **Every Critical Gate passes**, AND
2. **Total weighted score is at least 75/100**

Formula:

`isVeryHighlyAligned = (allCriticalGatesPassed == true) AND (totalScore >= 75)`

Evaluate every gate and every weighted rule internally even if an earlier critical gate fails.

Never increase a score merely to reach the 75-point threshold.

---

# CRITICAL GATES

Critical Gates do not contribute points.

Any failed gate forces:

`"isVeryHighlyAligned": false`

## GATE 1 — LOCATION ACCESSIBILITY / 100% WORK-FROM-HOME

PASS only when the role is explicitly confirmed as **100% remote / fully remote / work from home / virtual** and can be performed while residing in the candidate's documented region.

FAIL if:

* On-site work is required.
* Hybrid attendance is required.
* Periodic office attendance is required.
* The role is remote only within locations that exclude the candidate's documented region.
* Fully remote status cannot be confirmed.
* Location wording is materially ambiguous.

US-Remote roles pass unless another geographic restriction excludes the candidate's documented region.

---

## GATE 2 — EMPLOYMENT TYPE

FAIL if employment type is:

* Part-time
* Contract
* Temporary

PASS if:

* Full-time
* Full-time inferred from strong evidence
* Not Specified

---

## GATE 3 — RESIDENCY REQUIREMENTS

FAIL only if an explicit residency requirement excludes the candidate's documented location or region.

Examples:

* "Must reside in Texas"
* "Applicants must live in California"

If no residency restriction is stated, PASS.

---

## GATE 4 — SECURITY CLEARANCE

FAIL when all of the following are true:

1. A specific clearance is required,
2. It must already be held prior to hire,
3. That clearance is not documented in `myResume`, and
4. The JD does not state that it may be obtained after hire or that sponsorship is available.

Examples normally causing failure:

* Active Secret required
* Active Top Secret required
* Active TS/SCI required

PASS for:

* A clearance already documented in `myResume`
* No clearance requirement
* "Ability to obtain"
* Post-hire clearance eligibility
* Employer-sponsored clearance

---

## GATE 5 — CERTIFICATION REQUIREMENTS

FAIL if:

1. A specific certification is mandatory before hire,
2. the candidate does not already hold it according to `myResume`, and
3. The JD does not permit obtaining it after hire or during onboarding.

Preferred certifications do not trigger failure.

Always verify the candidate's existing certifications against `myResume`.

---

## GATE 6 — SALARY CEILING

Salary is a Critical Gate.

Evaluate the **maximum/upper bound of the stated annual base-salary range**, not the salary floor.

### PASS when:

* The stated annual salary ceiling is **at least $120,000**, even if the salary floor is below $120,000.
* Salary is **Not Specified**.
* The JD mentions compensation but does not provide an explicit or reliably determinable salary ceiling, such as only stating a starting salary or minimum salary.

Examples that PASS:

* `$100,000–$120,000`
* `$95,000–$135,000`
* `$120,000–$150,000`
* `Up to $120,000`
* Salary Not Specified
* `Starting at $105,000` with no stated maximum

### FAIL when:

* An explicit or reliably determinable annual salary ceiling is **less than $120,000**.

Examples that FAIL:

* `$90,000–$115,000`
* `$110,000–$119,999`
* `Up to $119,999`
* A single stated salary of `$115,000`

### Salary-Gate Interpretation Rules

* If only one salary number is provided as the role's salary, treat it as both the lower and upper bound.
* If an hourly rate or hourly range is provided, annualize using `hourlyRate × 2,080`; use the annualized upper bound for the gate.
* If multiple salary ranges are explicitly tied to different geographies and a candidate-location-applicable range is identifiable, evaluate the candidate-location-applicable ceiling.
* If multiple salary ranges are stated but geographic applicability is not identifiable, use the highest explicitly stated salary ceiling.
* Do not use bonuses, commissions, equity, stock, benefits, or other variable/ancillary compensation to raise a sub-$120,000 base-salary ceiling above the threshold unless the JD explicitly defines the quoted range itself as guaranteed salary.
* Do not fail merely because the salary floor is below $120,000.
* Do not fail a role solely because salary is absent.
* Do not infer an unstated salary ceiling.

Any Salary Gate failure forces:

`"isVeryHighlyAligned": false`

# WEIGHTED RULE 1 — EXPERIENCE YEARS ALIGNMENT

Maximum: **20 points**

Internally determine from `myResume`:

* Titled cybersecurity experience
* Total IT/technology experience
* Security-relevant functions performed in other roles

Score:

* **20:** the candidate's titled cybersecurity experience alone meets or exceeds the JD's relevant requirement.
* **18:** Requirement is slightly above titled cybersecurity experience, but strongly supported by broader IT experience plus documented security responsibilities.
* **15:** Requirement significantly exceeds titled cybersecurity experience, but the combined security experience, IT foundation, and documented achievements make candidacy credible.
* **12:** the candidate meets a broader IT experience requirement even though security-specific years are lower.
* **10:** Experience requirement is Not Specified.
* **8:** Requirement is moderately above the candidate's relevant experience and creates meaningful screening risk.
* **5:** Requirement is significantly above the candidate's relevant background and interview probability is low.
* **0:** Requirement is far beyond the candidate's career stage, such as 15–20+ years of directly relevant experience.

Important:

Do not count all IT years as cybersecurity years when the JD explicitly requires cybersecurity/security experience.

Consider whether a recruiter or ATS could reasonably screen the candidate out based on years alone.

# WEIGHTED RULE 4 — SALARY RANGE

Maximum: **10 points**

This weighted rule is separate from **GATE 6 — SALARY CEILING**.

Always evaluate the Salary Gate independently first, and continue calculating this weighted salary score even if the Salary Gate fails.

Use the upper bound of the stated annual salary range.

* **10:** ≥ $120,000
* **8:** $110,000–$119,999
* **6:** Salary Not Specified or no explicit/reliably determinable salary ceiling
* **4:** $100,000–$109,999
* **2:** $90,000–$99,999
* **0:** < $90,000

If only one salary number is provided as the role's salary, treat it as both the lower and upper bound.

If an hourly rate is provided, annualize using:

`hourlyRate × 2,080`

If multiple salary ranges are explicitly tied to different geographies and a candidate-location-applicable range is identifiable, use the candidate-location-applicable ceiling for both the gate and this weighted rule. If geographic applicability is not identifiable, use the highest explicitly stated salary ceiling.

Do not use bonuses, commissions, equity, stock, benefits, or other variable/ancillary compensation to inflate the salary ceiling unless the JD explicitly defines the quoted range itself as guaranteed salary.

Do not modify the score for geographic cost-of-living differences.

A weighted salary score below 10 does not itself determine alignment; however, any Salary Gate failure still forces `"isVeryHighlyAligned": false`.

# WEIGHTED RULE 5 — CULTURE & COMPANY FIT

Maximum: **10 points**

Evaluate only signals reasonably supported by the JD's wording.

* **10:** Strong positive indicators — collaborative, supportive, growth-oriented, reasonable expectations, professional development, work-life balance, or clear mission; no meaningful red flags.
* **8:** Neutral, standard corporate language with no meaningful positive or negative indicators.
* **6:** Minor concerns such as excessive buzzwords without broader evidence of dysfunction.
* **4:** Moderate concerns such as unrealistic breadth, excessive bureaucracy, micromanagement signals, or unreasonable expectations.
* **2:** Significant concerns suggesting high stress, rigidity, toxicity, or poor organizational health.
* **0:** Severe explicit red flags such as normalized extreme pressure, routine weekend expectations, no work-life balance, hostile tone, or obvious technological stagnation.

Do not infer company culture from stereotypes or unsupported assumptions.

# CONFIDENCE SCORE

Assign `confidence` based on the completeness and clarity of the available evidence.

* **0.90–1.00:** Comprehensive JD, clear requirements, strong evidence, unambiguous assessment
* **0.75–0.89:** Minor information gaps but sufficient evidence for a reliable assessment
* **0.60–0.74:** Important information is vague or missing, creating material uncertainty
* **Below 0.60:** Assessment is substantially constrained by missing or ambiguous information

Confidence measures certainty in the assessment, NOT strength of candidate alignment.

A poor match can still have very high confidence.
