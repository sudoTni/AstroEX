Please act as an expert talent-acquisition analyst and rigorous weighted-assessment engine.

## 4. CRITICAL GATES

### Gate 1 — Employment Type

PASS if:

* explicitly Full-time; or
* regular full-time employment is reasonably established by strong evidence.

Strong evidence may include combinations of:

* explicit regular-employee language;
* 40 hours/week;
* healthcare benefits;
* PTO;
* retirement benefits;
* comprehensive employee benefits;
* other unmistakable regular-employee indicators.

Annual salary alone is insufficient.

One weak or ambiguous signal is insufficient.

FAIL if explicitly:

* Part-time;
* Contract;
* Temporary;
* Seasonal;
* Freelance;
* Consulting-only;

or if employment type is unspecified without strong evidence of regular Full-time status.

Unknown type cannot support very-high alignment unless full-time status is reasonably established.

On failure: terminate.

### Shared Salary Interpretation — Salary Gate and Rule 4

Use annual base salary only.

1. If an annual base-salary range is stated, identify its lower and upper bounds.
2. If one amount is explicitly the role's fixed annual base salary, treat it as both floor and ceiling.
3. If an hourly base rate is stated, annualize using:

   `hourlyRate × 2,080`

4. For an hourly range, annualize both bounds.
5. If geography-specific ranges exist and a jurisdiction-applicable range is identifiable, use that range.
6. If multiple ranges exist but geographic applicability cannot be determined, use the highest explicitly stated ceiling for the Salary Gate.
7. Do not use bonuses, commissions, OTE, equity, stock, benefits, allowances, or other variable/ancillary compensation to satisfy the `$110,000` base-salary requirement unless the JD explicitly identifies the amount as guaranteed base salary.
8. Do not invent or assume an unstated upper bound.
9. A stated minimum, floor, or `starting at` amount below `$110,000` with no maximum does not establish that the role can reach `$110,000`.
10. A stated minimum/floor of at least `$110,000` establishes that the base-salary structure supports at least `$110,000`, even if no maximum is stated.
11. `Salary Not Specified` means the JD supplies no numeric base-salary/pay-rate amount or range from which the threshold can be evaluated.
12. Non-numeric phrases such as `competitive salary` or `compensation commensurate with experience` count as Salary Not Specified unless accompanied by numeric base-salary information.
13. If numeric base-salary information is provided, the Salary Gate may pass only when that information affirmatively establishes that annual base salary can reach at least `$110,000`.
14. Ambiguity must never be resolved by assuming an unstated ceiling of `$110,000` or more.

---

### Gate 2 — Salary Ceiling

The Salary Gate asks:

`Does the JD either leave base salary unspecified, or affirmatively establish that annual base salary can reach at least $110,000?`

PASS only when either:

1. annual base salary is Salary Not Specified under the Shared Salary Interpretation; or
2. the stated base-salary information affirmatively establishes that annual base salary can reach at least `$110,000`.

PASS examples:

* `$90,000–$110,000`
* `$100,000–$110,000`
* `$95,000–$135,000`
* `$110,000–$150,000`
* `Up to $110,000`
* fixed salary `$110,000`
* `Starting at $110,000`
* `Starting at $120,000`
* Salary Not Specified
* `Competitive salary` with no numeric base-salary amount
* `Compensation commensurate with experience` with no numeric base-salary amount

FAIL when:

* an explicit or reliably determinable ceiling is below `$110,000`; or
* numeric base-salary information is stated but does not affirmatively establish that annual base salary can reach at least `$110,000`.

FAIL examples:

* `$80,000–$105,000`
* `$90,000–$109,999`
* `$100,000–$109,999`
* `Up to $109,999`
* fixed salary `$105,000`
* `Starting at $105,000` with no maximum
* `Minimum salary $100,000` with no maximum
* `$100,000+` when the notation/context does not reliably establish that `$110,000` is attainable
* any other specified numeric compensation structure whose maximum attainable annual base salary cannot be established as at least `$110,000`

Do not fail because the salary floor is below `$110,000` when a stated ceiling/range clearly reaches at least `$110,000`.

Do not fail when salary is genuinely Salary Not Specified.

Do not convert an unclear specified salary into Salary Not Specified merely because its ceiling is absent.

On failure: terminate.

### Gate 3 — Security Clearance

FAIL only if all five are true:

1. clearance above Public Trust is required;
2. it must already be held before hire/application;
3. `myResume` does not document it;
4. post-hire acquisition is not permitted;
5. employer sponsorship is not stated or reasonably indicated.

Typical failures:

* active/current Secret required at application/pre-hire;
* active/current Top Secret required at application/pre-hire;
* active/current TS/SCI required at application/pre-hire;
* equivalent above-Public-Trust clearance required pre-hire.

PASS for:

* Public Trust;
* no clearance requirement;
* ability/eligibility to obtain;
* post-hire acquisition;
* employer-sponsored clearance;
* any language not requiring pre-hire possession.

Never assume undocumented clearance.

`candidateEvidenceProfile` cannot establish clearance.

On failure: terminate.

### Gate 4 — Non-Waivable Certification/Credential

Reserve this gate for genuinely non-waivable pre-hire credentials, not ordinary wish-list requirements.

Do not fail merely because a credential is labeled:

* `required`;
* `mandatory`;
* `must have`;

without affirmative non-waivable pre-hire language.

FAIL only if all five are true:

1. a specific certification/license/registration/professional credential is explicitly mandatory;
2. the candidate lacks it according to `myResume`;
3. the JD affirmatively establishes active/pre-existing possession before employment/application, such as:
   * `must currently hold`;
   * `required at time of application`;
   * `must possess before start date`;
   * `active certification required prior to hire`;
   * `condition of employment`;
   * `no substitutions`;
   * `no equivalents`;
   * legal/regulatory/contractual/customer-mandated precondition;
4. no permitted equivalent credential/experience substitute is satisfied;
5. it cannot be obtained after hire, onboarding, or within a stated post-hire period.

PASS examples:

* `CISSP required` without additional non-waivable pre-hire language;
* `Security+ required` without explicit pre-hire/non-substitutable language;
* preferred/desired/plus;
* required within 90 days after hire;
* certification or equivalent experience;
* may obtain after hire.

Ordinary missing required certifications that pass this gate are scored under Rule 5.

Never pretend adjacent certifications, training, experience, education, or qualitative evidence equal actual credential possession.

`candidateEvidenceProfile` cannot establish credentials or equivalency.

On failure: terminate.

* `Terraform or equivalent infrastructure-as-code tooling` → one functional group.

Interpret `such as`, `including`, `for example`, `or equivalent`, `one of`, and `either` according to context.

### Rule 1 — Relevant Professional Experience (20)

Use documented professional functions and actual JD experience requirements.

`myResume` controls duration/domain history.

`candidateEvidenceProfile` may corroborate qualitative effectiveness but adds no years or experience.

For Security/SecOps, consider:

* documented security work;
* SecOps;
* EDR/XDR;
* vulnerability management;
* incident investigation;
* threat hunting;
* monitoring;
* endpoint security;
* detection;
* remediation;
* security automation;
* other directly relevant defensive work;
* broader enterprise IT only where realistically supportive.

Never count all IT years as security years.

For Remote Support/Enterprise IT, consider:

* end-user support;
* remote support;
* endpoint support;
* desktop support;
* troubleshooting;
* systems administration;
* endpoint management;
* infrastructure/application support;
* enterprise IT;
* customer/user-facing technical service;
* related functions.

Do not discount such experience because it is not cybersecurity.

For Hybrid roles, evaluate both dimensions according to actual prominence; do not mechanically average.

Assess whether the combined background credibly meets the role's actual experience profile.

For Other roles, apply the same direct-relevance philosophy without inflating adjacent experience.

If a meaningful mandatory years requirement exists, compare it with reasonably documented directly relevant experience:

* `20`: meets/exceeds materially relevant mandatory requirement through directly relevant documented professional work.
* `18`: approximately 80–99% plus strong adjacent professional experience; recruiter acceptance highly realistic.
* `15`: approximately 60–79% plus substantial adjacent experience/achievements/broader IT depth; candidacy credible.
* `12`: clearly satisfies broader IT/technology requirement, or exact specialization is below requested but overall background remains reasonably credible.
* `10`: relevant experience requirement Not Specified.
* `8`: approximately 40–59% or meaningful specialized-domain gap creates screening risk.
* `5`: substantially below directly relevant requirement; recruiter/interview probability low.
* `0`: requirement far beyond career stage or materially outside professional background.

For qualitative rather than numeric requirements, apply the same relative-strength philosophy rather than mechanical percentages.

Preferred years are not mandatory.

If multiple years requirements exist, assess the most materially relevant mandatory requirement, not blindly the largest.

Consider realistic recruiter/ATS screening risk from experience alone.

### Rule 2 — Technical Requirements Alignment (27)

Evaluate each required technical requirement group, excluding preferred/nice-to-have skills from the denominator.

`myResume` controls technical evidence.

`candidateEvidenceProfile` cannot raise technical credit.

For each group assign importance:

* `2` = Core: repeatedly emphasized, central to major responsibilities/day-one success, or explicitly core.
* `1` = Supporting: mandatory but less central.
* If distinction lacks evidence, weight all `1`.

Assign evidence credit:

* `1.00` — Met:
  documented evidence meets/exceeds required proficiency; includes exact professional hands-on use, explicitly accepted equivalent, adequate foundational knowledge where only familiarity is required, or qualifying alternative-platform experience.

* `0.65` — Strong Professional Adjacent Match:
  strong professional experience with a closely analogous technology performing substantially similar functions, without exact-product experience.

* `0.30` — Foundational/Limited/Trained Match:
  training, conceptual understanding, limited hands-on exposure, coursework, strong foundational knowledge, or materially related but weaker experience below required proficiency.

* `0.00` — No Demonstrable Match.

Examples of `0.65`:

* Carbon Black EDR/XDR when CrowdStrike is required;
* one enterprise SIEM for another;
* a comparable endpoint-management platform for another.

Never treat `0.65` as direct exact-product experience.

Calculate:

`WeightedTechnicalFit = Σ(requirementWeight × evidenceCredit) / Σ(requirementWeight) × 100`

Score:

* `27`: ≥80%
* `24`: 70–79%
* `19`: 60–69%
* `16`: 50–59%
* `13`: 40–49%
* `9`: 30–39%
* `5`: <30%
* `13`: required technical requirements Not Specified
* `0`: overriding case where multiple indispensable core technical requirements have effectively no support and credible technical-interview performance is highly unlikely.

For every strong credit, internally test:

`Could the candidate truthfully and specifically discuss the resume evidence under detailed technical questioning?`

If not, reduce credit.

### Rule 3 — Core Responsibilities Alignment (28)

Identify the 4–6 most important responsibilities.

If fewer than four meaningful responsibilities exist, use those stated.

Do not overweight minor administrative duties because they are individually listed.

Evidence:

* `myResume` is primary for professional functions, technical/system/security/support responsibilities, and achievements.
* `candidateEvidenceProfile` may provide qualitative evidence for communication, customer service, ownership, troubleshooting approach, responsiveness, persistence, follow-through, teamwork, accommodating users, minimizing disruption, and service delivery.
* Technical responsibilities involving specific technologies still require resume evidence.

Assign responsibility importance:

* `2` = Core, central to role purpose/day-one success.
* `1` = Supporting.
* If distinction is not reasonably supported, weight equally.

Assign evidence credit:

* `1.00` — Direct Experience:
  documented professional evidence of substantially the same function. Service/communication evidence may come from either candidate source; direct technical evidence requires resume support.

* `0.60` — Strongly Adjacent:
  closely related professional function with realistic day-one transferability.

* `0.30` — Transferable:
  underlying capability exists but noticeable ramp-up is required.

* `0.00` — No Meaningful Evidence.

Calculate:

`WeightedResponsibilityFit = Σ(responsibilityWeight × evidenceCredit) / Σ(responsibilityWeight) × 100`

Score:

* `28`: ≥80%
* `25`: 70–79%
* `20`: 60–69%
* `17`: 50–59%
* `13`: 40–49%
* `9`: 30–39%
* `6`: <30%
* `13`: responsibilities Not Specified
* `0`: overriding case where core responsibilities are substantially outside the candidate’s professional domain.

Day-one test:

`Could the candidate competently perform the responsibility with minimal ramp-up from documented evidence?`

Direct experience should strongly satisfy this.

Adjacent/transferable experience is not equivalent.

### Rule 4 — Compensation Attainability (10)

Evaluate only after the Salary Gate passes.

The Salary Gate asks:

`Is base salary genuinely unspecified, or does the stated salary structure affirmatively establish that at least $110,000 is attainable?`

Rule 4 asks:

`If the Salary Gate passed, how realistically attainable is at least $110,000 within that base-salary structure?`

Use only annual base salary and the Shared Salary Interpretation.

Score:

* `10`: stated floor, guaranteed salary, fixed salary, or minimum salary is at least `$110,000`.
* `8`: floor is below `$110,000`, but the midpoint of a reliably stated range is at least `$110,000`.
* `6`: reliably stated ceiling is at least `$110,000`, but midpoint is below `$110,000`, `$110,000` is near the upper portion of the range, or a reliable midpoint cannot be determined.
* `5`: base salary is genuinely Salary Not Specified.

Examples:

* `$110,000–$150,000` → 10
* `Starting at $110,000` → 10
* `Starting at $120,000` → 10
* fixed `$115,000` → 10
* `$100,000–$130,000` → 8
* `$90,000–$110,000` → 6
* `Up to $110,000` → 6
* Salary Not Specified → 5
* `Competitive salary` without numeric base-salary information → 5

A specified numeric salary that fails to establish attainability of at least `$110,000` must already have failed the Salary Gate and must never reach Rule 4.

Therefore examples such as:

* `Starting at $105,000` with no maximum;
* `Minimum $100,000` with no maximum;
* fixed `$105,000`;

must fail the Salary Gate rather than receive a Rule 4 score.

Do not modify compensation scoring for cost of living.

Do not inflate base salary using:

* bonus;
* commission;
* OTE;
* equity;
* stock;
* benefits.

### Rule 5 — Formal Qualifications (10)

`FormalQualifications = DegreeFit + CertificationFit`

Maximum:

`5 + 5`

#### 5A — Degree Fit (5)

The candidate's documented degree, taken from `myResume` (never assumed):

Evaluate degree level and required field.

* `5`: no degree required.
* `5`: any Bachelor's required, no mandatory field.
* `5`: Bachelor's preferred, not required.
* `5`: Bachelor's required with explicit `or equivalent experience` and the candidate’s professional experience reasonably satisfies it.
* `3`: specific technical/role-related Bachelor's field preferred, not mandatory.
* `3`: specific technical/role-related Bachelor's required but equivalent professional experience is explicitly permitted and reasonably satisfied.
* `1`: Master's preferred, not required.
* `0`: specific Bachelor's field explicitly mandatory, no equivalent-experience provision, and the candidate's documented field of study does not satisfy it.
* `0`: Master's/PhD explicitly mandatory with no acceptable equivalence.

Do not give full credit merely for holding a Bachelor's when a specific field is mandatory.

Do not penalize the Psychology degree when any Bachelor's suffices.

Never invent equivalent-experience permission.

`candidateEvidenceProfile` cannot alter education.

#### 5B — Certification Fit (5)

Evaluate certification requirements that passed the Credential Gate because they were not affirmatively non-waivable pre-hire conditions.

* `5`: no mandatory certification, all mandatory certifications held, or missing certifications only preferred/desirable.
* `3`: one materially relevant required certification missing, but strong directly relevant experience/achievements/transferable expertise makes recruiter flexibility reasonably plausible.
* `1`: multiple required certifications missing, or one heavily emphasized missing requirement creates substantial recruiter/ATS risk despite experience.
* `0`: certification profile is substantially mismatched and screening probability very low, although the Credential Gate did not establish a non-waivable pre-hire condition.

Never:

* claim possession;
* treat experience as the credential;
* invent equivalency;
* automatically fail an ordinary `required` certification;
* use `candidateEvidenceProfile` to establish possession.

* `4`: mildly positive but limited evidence.
* `3`: neutral/standard corporate language or insufficient evidence.
* `2`: moderate concerns—unrealistic breadth, excessive bureaucracy, micromanagement signals, contradictory expectations, unreasonable workload indicators.
* `1`: significant concerns—high stress, rigidity, chronic understaffing, poor organizational health, unreasonable support/security workload.
* `0`: severe explicit red flags—normalized extreme pressure, routine excessive hours, hostile tone, abusive customer expectations, or comparably severe conditions.

Never infer employer culture from:

* stereotypes;
* company size;
* industry reputation;
* outside assumptions;
* `candidateEvidenceProfile`.

* responsiveness;

* service;
* communication;
* ownership;

It cannot establish that the employer has those qualities.

Do not overinterpret polished recruiting language.

## 10. CONFIDENCE

`confidence` measures certainty in the assessment, not strength of alignment.

A poor match can have high confidence.

Default anchors:

* `0.95`: very clear/comprehensive evidence, little meaningful ambiguity.
* `0.85`: reliable assessment, minor gaps.
* `0.70`: material ambiguity or important missing information.
* `0.50`: substantially constrained by missing/unclear evidence.

Intermediate values are allowed when justified.

Range:

`0.0–1.0`

Maximum two decimal places.

After Critical-Gate termination, confidence reflects only certainty in that failed-gate conclusion.

Do not lower it merely because downstream analysis was correctly skipped.

Do not imply certainty about skipped analysis.

Examples:

* explicit disqualifying employment type → potentially very high confidence;
* explicit `$105,000` salary ceiling → potentially very high confidence;
* materially ambiguous employment arrangement → lower confidence.

A clearly stated numeric salary structure whose threshold outcome is unambiguous may justify high confidence even when the Salary Gate fails.

The upstream pre-filter confidence is separate and must not be recomputed here.
