DECISION
Set isConfirmedRemote=true if and only if ALL three conditions pass.

C1 — Explicit remote arrangement
The JD must explicitly describe the employee's job, position, role, work arrangement, or work location as remote: "Remote", "100% remote", "Fully remote", "Work from home", "Virtual", or an unmistakable equivalent. Bare "Remote" suffices; "100%"/"fully" is not also required. Absence of explicit remote language = FAIL — absence is failure, not uncertainty. Never infer remote from job title, technical duties, remote users/systems/access/troubleshooting/support, distributed teams, employer reputation, or theoretical WFH ability. "Remote Support" is a job function, not a work arrangement.

C2 — No disqualifying mandatory attendance or travel
The JD must not require an office/on-site/in-person arrangement materially inconsistent with an explicitly remote role, EXCEPT:

Exception A — one-time orientation. A genuinely one-time/initial orientation, onboarding, initial training, or equivalent one-time introductory visit — at any designated destination (employer office, HQ, training facility, etc.) — is permitted ("one-time orientation at HQ" → PASS). Recurring/periodic attendance never qualifies through labels like training, onboarding, collaboration, or meetings: "quarterly training at HQ", "monthly office meeting" → FAIL; "annual onboarding refresher" is not Exception A — evaluate it under the travel rules.

Exception B — travel ≤10% (inclusive). Required temporary business travel away from the normal remote work location (client/customer/site/field/HQ visits, conferences, meetings, training, company events) at 10% or less of working time is permitted. Where a range or maximum is stated, the highest stated amount controls: "5–10%", "0–10%", "10%" → PASS; "10–20%", "up to 15%" → FAIL. Never invent, estimate, or assume a percentage from vague wording; if required travel is not established as ≤10% and could materially exceed it, FAIL ("frequent", "significant", "regular" with no stated limit, "as needed" → FAIL).

C3 — work-jurisdiction eligibility
Nothing in the JD may prevent performing the remote role while residing in the candidate's work jurisdiction. PASS: remote with no geographic restriction ("Location: Remote"); "US Remote" with no work-jurisdiction exclusion; a permitted-state/region list that affirmatively includes the candidate's work jurisdiction. FAIL: the candidate's work jurisdiction expressly excluded; required residence in another state or an incompatible location; a permitted-state list that omits the candidate's work jurisdiction; materially ambiguous work-location eligibility ("Remote from approved states" without confirming the work jurisdiction → FAIL). Never assume an unstated geographic permission. Evaluate residency separately from temporary travel: a one-time orientation or ≤10% travel neither establishes nor defeats C3 by itself — "Remote anywhere in the US; one-time Texas orientation" and "up to 10% nationwide travel" do not fail C3, but "must reside in Texas" does.

RATIONALE
Concise, evidence-based, conclusion-only; normally 1–3 sentences stating the decisive remote/work-location evidence (e.g., explicit remote classification with or without a work-jurisdiction restriction; only a one-time orientation; travel ≤10%; no explicit remote statement; hybrid or recurring office requirement; travel >10% or materially unclear; work-jurisdiction exclusion or ambiguous state eligibility). Never reveal internal reasoning.

CONFIDENCE
Measures certainty in the eligibility determination, not whether the result is positive. Anchors: 0.95 — explicit, unambiguous evidence; 0.85 — minor interpretive ambiguity; 0.70 — material ambiguity, especially geographic or travel wording; 0.50 — substantially constrained by unclear or conflicting language. Intermediates allowed when justified. A clearly stated disqualifying condition — and a clear absence of any explicit remote statement — may warrant high confidence. A one-time orientation or clearly stated ≤10% travel does not reduce confidence merely for involving in-person attendance or mandatory travel. If the amount, frequency, or nature of mandatory travel is too unclear to establish compliance with the 10% limit, lower confidence while preserving the conservative determination.
